Year by year, 2020 to 2025

Each year Steam names its 12 highest-grossing games, in no particular order. Steam doesn’t publish the rank within those 12 or any revenue figures, so this shows who made the top 12, not who came first.

How the history was checked

Steam’s year-end pages load their lists with scripts that couldn’t be read directly, so each year’s top 12 was taken from press coverage and only kept where independent outlets agreed on all 12 games. The weekly charts were read straight from Steam.

Where these numbers come from

Top-seller rank, price, discount, rank change and weeks on chart come from Steam’s live Top Sellers chart for the United States, which Steam ranks by revenue. It was read once, on Sep 28, 2026 at about 01:05 UTC.

Current players and today’s peak come from Steam’s official most-played chart (ISteamChartsService), as mirrored by Steam Dashboard at 03:15 UTC on Sep 27, 2026. That’s about 22 hours older than the sales chart.

What’s missing, and why

Reviews, Metacritic, genres, tags, developers, release dates, owner estimates, and copies-sold or revenue estimates weren’t retrievable for this snapshot, so they show as Data unavailable rather than guesses.

The 7-day and 30-day views compare today’s live chart with Steam’s weekly charts. There’s no 24-hour or 90-day chart to compare against, and the year-end lists aren’t ranked, so those views stay empty. Game artwork isn’t shown because the page can’t load remote images.

Titles matched by App ID

Some games on the players chart were listed only by App ID. Where the ID belongs to a long-established game, the title was matched by reference and is underlined with dots. Six IDs couldn’t be matched and stay as App numbers.
